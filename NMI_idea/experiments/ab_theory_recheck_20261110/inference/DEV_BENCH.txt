DEV-only continuous SMC smoke evidence (corrected CESS)
========================================================

This is a local geometric smoke bench, not a closed-loop result and not a
held-out performance claim.  It uses only sample_prior(720901, 128) and
sample_worlds(720902, 1), with eight MH sweeps per tempering stage.

The previous version used ordinary post-update ESS in the CESS denominator;
that snapshot is preserved in ``before_cess_fix/``.  The current implementation
uses N*(sum(w exp(delta*l)))^2 / sum(w exp(2*delta*l)).

All five rows now reach beta=1 without changing the configured max-20 limit.
The same public frame keys and DEV seeds were used for the corrected rerun;
the final row has 127 unique geometries after a legitimate resample.  These
are only local geometric smoke results, not closed-loop or held-out claims.

The local contract suite reports 12 passed tests, including the nonuniform-
weight CESS formula, two consecutive synthetic continuous blocks, hard-zero
support handling, duplicate-frame rollback, and the near-one-beta completion
guard.

Performance/parity update
-------------------------
The full-history and new-block callbacks now form one union of unique poses
and call ``predict_batch`` once per current/proposal population.  The two LL
vectors remain separate, so beta still applies only to the new block.  On the
same five-probe DEV run, NumPy took 22.424 s total and CUDA float64 took
12.651 s, about 1.77x faster.  Stages, beta endpoints, ESS and acceptance
values matched to the displayed precision; MH particle trajectories are not
claimed bitwise identical.

The CUDA predictor was compared against the unchanged NumPy environment on
720901 particles and public frames rendered from 720902.  Position/depth/
length differences were below 1e-10 m, source/visibility/detection and
identity fields were below 1e-10, the largest angle difference was
1.56e-7 rad, and the largest finite public-frame LL difference was
2.16e-10 (within the registered 1e-6 LL gate).  CUDA is opt-in through
``prediction_backend='cuda'``; NumPy remains the default.
