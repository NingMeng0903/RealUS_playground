Bounded primary-source check, 2026-11-11

Purpose: establish the actual novelty threshold for the two proposed mechanisms.
This is not an exhaustive priority search or a claim that no related work exists.
Only publisher/proceedings pages and author manuscripts/projects are relied on.
The experimental design recommendations below are the auditor's inferences.

1. Li, Lai, Li, Zhang & Pan, "Uncalibrated Adaptive Robot Visual Servoing on
Image Space with Parameter Convergence", ACC 2024, pp.3088-3093.
https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10644769/
Publisher abstract verified; full paper was not accessible in this check.
The abstract reports composite adaptation, parameter and image-error convergence
under interval excitation, and a 7-DoF robot experiment. Therefore generic online
Jacobian/parameter identification plus improved visual servoing is established
prior art. Our own linear derivation does not depend on an uninspected proof in
this paper. Novelty must concern the anatomy-conditioned observation model,
task-relevant identifiable quantities, or demonstrable transfer/data savings.

2. S. L. Li et al., "Controlling diverse robots by inferring Jacobian fields
with deep networks", Nature 643, 89-95 (2025), published 25 June 2025.
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09170-0
Author manuscript read: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08722v2
Author project: https://scenerepresentations.org/publications/jacobian-fields/
The work learns a visual representation of geometry and action sensitivity
from random robot motions and uses it for closed-loop control. It demonstrates
different robot systems; that is not evidence for our anatomical transfer.
It already establishes the broad response-field idea. A defensible distinction
here needs independent patient/condition transfer, partial internal visibility,
identity ambiguity and calibrated task-response prediction at lower adaptation
cost. Calling a response model a field or reusable skill is insufficient.
Metadata note: the author HTML has stale template conference/year placeholders;
the publication year/venue above were checked against the publisher and author
project, not inferred from those placeholders.

3. Raina et al., "Robotic Sonographer: Autonomous Robotic Ultrasound using
Domain Expertise in Bayesian Optimization", ICRA 2023.
https://arxiv.org/html/2307.02442v1
The author paper uses an expert-informed prior quality map, Gaussian-process
Bayesian optimization and image-quality feedback, evaluated on three bladder
phantoms. It directly shows that anatomical/domain priors and probabilistic
search can coexist. It does not establish our continuous vascular response
transfer or branch-dependent acquisition. Include a competent prior-informed
BO/coverage baseline if claiming acquisition/search novelty; do not characterize
existing probabilistic methods as requiring removal of templates or all priors.

4. Vinter-Hviid, Sloth, Savarimuthu & Iturrate, "Safe contact-based robot active
search using Bayesian optimization and control barrier functions", 2024.
https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2024.1344367/full
The primary article combines informative continuous expected improvement with
contact-force control for ultrasound search, reporting simulation and robot
experiments. It already treats information along continuous sampling paths and
bounded search time. Thus endpoint-only sensing baselines and uncharged contact/
motion would be inadequate here. It does not substitute for the proposed test
of posterior-dependent, state-dependent subsequent imaging actions. Compare
continuous-information acquisition fairly, and preserve our current distinction
between geometric simulation and actual force/ultrasound evidence.

5. Curtis et al., "Partially Observable Task and Motion Planning with
Uncertainty and Risk Awareness" (TAMPURA), RSS 2024.
Official proceedings: https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss20/p118.html
Author full text read: https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10454v2
The method plans over belief-space controllers with preconditions and uncertain
effects, combining information gathering, task utility and continuous execution.
Its comparisons give planners the same controllers; Appendix H fixes simulated
controller-query budgets. Appendix J states an abstraction stationarity condition
and acknowledges imperfect abstractions. Consequently, "information matters
because it changes feasible future actions" is not new. A contribution here
would be a concrete anatomical response/decision abstraction with tested validity,
or measured computational/data benefit over an equally capable belief planner.
Our inference is not that TAMPURA has already solved ultrasound or our exact
response-identifiability problem.

6. Hu et al., "Real-World Reinforcement Learning of Active Perception
Behaviors", NeurIPS 2025 camera-ready author version.
https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01188v1
The paper trains active perception policies using privileged observations for
training-time critics while withholding them from deployed policies; it reports
eight simulated/real task settings. This establishes a relevant learned-policy
comparison class and the need to separate training privileges from deployment.
It does not require this project to adopt RL. If claiming general superiority
over learned active perception, omitting a competent history-conditioned policy
forever is not defensible. A known-anatomy test-time oracle remains a privileged
diagnostic here, never a legal success result.

Foundational theory boundary (not presented as recent novelty)

Subramanian, Sinha, Seraj & Mahajan, "Approximate Information State for
Approximate Planning and Reinforcement Learning in Partially Observed Systems",
JMLR 23(12), 2022.
https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/20-1165.html
https://jmlr.org/papers/volume23/20-1165/20-1165.pdf
Information-state conditions preserve current rewards and future predictions;
approximate conditions lead to bounded planning loss. Thus a new name for a
belief summary, a Bellman backup, or a generic approximation bound is insufficient.
The project must show which anatomy-specific summary has identifiable, accurate
predictive content and nonvacuous decision error under actual costs and actions.

cross-patient transfer. Those are separate empirical questions.
