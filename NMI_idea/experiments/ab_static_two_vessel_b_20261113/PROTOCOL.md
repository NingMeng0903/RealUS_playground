# Static artery–vein mechanism test

This test uses the complete two-view replanning controller as the comparison for a first decision that anticipates a conditional second acquisition. The task is always positive. Correct completion requires two newly acquired images at the prescribed adjacent stations, with a selected candidate belonging to the same registered artery in each. A vein can be mistaken for the artery. Old images, failed acquisitions, wrong selections, and abstentions do not count as success.

## Frozen environment

- Retarget the `Artery` and `Vein` meshes together using the existing Among_US Blender and canonical SMPL-X assets. Pose both at the archived static 15° knee transform. Use the actual probe planes at stations 3, 4 and 5 with views 3, 7 and 8. At each plane and registration offset select the unique closed contour within 45 mm of the archived reference line for each vessel mesh. Use its polygon centroid. Archive the section radii for geometry inspection. Neither vessel has pulsation, flow, compression or role-dependent detection. Exclude other vessels and false-positive emitters.
- Registration shifts affect both vessels together. Use five offsets per axis from −2 to +2 standard deviations. The axes are lateral and inward at the central probe at station 3. The discretized Gaussian prior uses 2.3 mm lateral scale and 1.7 mm inward scale, inherited nuisance magnitudes from `ab_knee_latent_rl_20261112/knee_world.py`. They are simulation assumptions. Evaluate exactly on all 25 shifts.
- Use the inherited 50 mm lateral field and 3–52 mm depth window. The two vessels share 0.84 intrinsic detection and Gaussian centroid noise (1.0 mm lateral, 0.68 mm depth). Truncate each noise coordinate at six standard deviations and normalize to make the numerically negligible tails explicit. Each return outside the aperture is censored. Within it use the same fixed, approximately 10 mm bins for both vessels and all poses. Expose the unordered multiset of binned returns, including no returns and equal-bin duplicates. Duplicate ordering is randomized for evaluation only. No raw ultrasound, U-Net, registration drift or learned classifier is used.
- Use only the nine `dird_top` poses and 63 old checked directed/hold edges restricted to these poses. Integrate the archived trajectory costs for every acquisition, including holds. Each edge has 1.50 s simulated motion/acquisition time. Report actual simulated TCP travel, turning, rail travel, time and captures. Planner time is a separate ledger. Robot–patient contact and collision are outside the archived edge check.
- Shared prescan starts at central station 4 and acquires at station 4 then station 3. Exact expected posterior mass concentration chooses views over the remaining fixed coverage order, with actual posterior update and view replanning after the first acquisition. Prefix scoring uses the inherited motion prices from the earlier prescan. All arms start the suffix at the same acquired history, posterior and pose. Prefix costs are included in total ledgers.

## Shared task and comparisons

Use the inherited suffix limits of 65 mm TCP, 90° turn and 250 mm rail, with at most three new frames and 4.50 s of modeled motion. The first fresh acquisition may be at station 3 or 4. If another diagnostic acquisition is needed, it must be at station 4 so the final station 5 image can directly follow it. The qualifying station 4 image counts toward the task. The terminal choice uses the maximum *joint* posterior probability that both selected returns are the artery, with the inherited 0.87 threshold applied to this pair. Optimize probability of correct accepted completion with the same threshold and legal edges for every policy. Report misidentification separately. No claim of empirical probability calibration follows from this model.

- **MPC.** At the shared exit, forecasts either the single acquisition followed by the common finishing rule, or a fixed second diagnostic view. It forecasts the common far view and declaration conditional on the public packets. After the actual first packet it reoptimizes whether and where to acquire a second diagnostic or continue. It receives every observation and can stop.
- **B.** Uses the same tree and moves the choice of whether and where to take a second diagnostic inside the expectation over the first packet. All later decisions use the same replanner as MPC.
- **Fixed first.** A fixed acoustic view type at station 4 followed by the identical live MPC. Evaluate all three types. Report the strongest fixed control across the declared prior, an optimistic model-based control with no choice conditioned on the prescan packet.
- **Two images.** A common adaptive one-diagnostic policy followed by a second new image at station 5, with full observation updates and the same acceptance. This measures what the optional third acquisition can achieve.

Compute `F(a)` for MPC's forecast and `G(a)` for the same first view followed by realized replanning. Compare `max G − G(a_MPC)`. Integrate over the prescan distribution and report how much exit probability has different first actions, meaningful gains, or genuinely different preferred second actions. Branches with individual prescan mass below `1e-12` are listed and their aggregate mass bounds their effect on each reported expected success and success difference. A root agreement with common tie handling yields identical live suffix decisions on paired tapes. Optimize success under the common modeled acceptance rule. Score wrong declarations and resources from the same terminal paths.

The previous benchmark used five percentage points as the target effect. A finite-model success ceiling below a five-point advantage closes that target for this restricted action/sensor class. The declared simulated resource equivalence bands are ±2 mm TCP, ±2° turn, ±5 mm rail and ±0.10 s. A positive gap alone does not establish matched cost. A short independent paired execution replay checks bookkeeping, sampling and realized replanning. Further perturbation or learning is useful only if the smallest model has actionable headroom.
