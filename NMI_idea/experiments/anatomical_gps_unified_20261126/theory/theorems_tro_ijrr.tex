\paragraph{Model.}
On a compact metric graph $\G$ with the vascular landmark vertices, let
$h_v\sim\mathcal N(\bar h_v,\sigma_h^2)$ independently ($\sigma_h=25\,\mathrm{mm}$)
and $h_e(\tau)=(1-\tau)h_u+\tau h_v$ along oriented edge $e=(u,v)$.
Use a common material chart $\xi=(\theta,h,d)$, locally lifted from the circle in $\theta$.
In a first-order displacement model with independent component priors, write
\[
 c_e=\Phi_{\beta,q}(\bar\xi_e)+G_{\beta,q}(\bar\xi_e)
 \big[u_s(h_e)+\delta_e(\tau)+g_e(\tau,u_t;\kappa_e)+z_{e,t}\big].
\]
Incident edges share the same nominal endpoint/frame and the same endpoint
values of $g$ and $z$ (edge-specific terms may instead vanish there).
The shared field $u_s$ is Mat\'ern-$3/2$ with $\ell_s=180\,\mathrm{mm}$ and
$\sigma_s=35\,\mathrm{mm}$. The relative field is defined by the Kirchhoff
Laplacian, with covariance $a^2(\kappa^2-\Delta_{\G})^{-2}$,
$\kappa=1/(45\,\mathrm{mm})$, and $a$ scaled to a knot-average variance of
$(6\,\mathrm{mm})^2$ on the reference discretization. Kirchhoff eigenfunctions
have continuous vertex traces and zero total outward derivative (Neumann at
leaves). A shared vertex value does not impose pointwise balance of piecewise
linear slopes for every finite-element draw.

\begin{theorem}[Material gauge and discrete graph precision]\label{thm:graph}
At a common station $h$, a difference of aligned material residuals removes
$u_s(h)$ exactly. In a common output frame, the linearized pose Jacobian of
a relative difference has norm at most $L_q\|\xi_a-\xi_b\|$ if $J_q$ is
$L_q$-Lipschitz in the chart. On a bounded-degree graph with lumped linear
finite elements and an augmented Mat\'ern state, the joint prior precision
has $O(N)$ nonzeros. For systems of bounded elimination width the Gaussian
factorization and solve cost is $O(N)$. Raw simulation draws from that same
conditional prior have zero prior KL divergence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract $G_e^{-1}(c_e-\Phi_e)$ at the two vessels. In the world frame the
uncancelled shared term would be $(G_a-G_b)u_s(h)$.
The first-order pose term is $(J_q(\xi_a)-J_q(\xi_b))\Delta q$;
apply the Lipschitz inequality. If $D$ is the lumped mass and $K$ the
stiffness, define $B=\kappa^2D+K$. For independent standard normals $\epsilon$,
$B\delta=aD^{1/2}\epsilon$ gives $\Lambda_{\G}=a^{-2}BD^{-1}B$ by inverting its
covariance. The same degree of freedom is used on every edge incident to a
vertex; the stiffness uses the Kirchhoff weak form. The sparsity pattern has
at most two-hop entries in the knot graph. For the shared chain, with
$\lambda=\sqrt{3}/\ell_s$, keep $x_i=(u_s(h_i),u_s'(h_i))$ on distinct
ordered knots fixed independently of the latent landmarks.
The stationary transition is $F_i=\exp(A\Delta h_i)$, where
$A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-\lambda^2&-2\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
and the innovation covariance is $P_\infty-F_i P_\infty F_i^\top$, with
$P_\infty=\diag(\sigma_s^2,\sigma_s^2\lambda^2)$. The initial density and
pair factors give block tridiagonal precision. Independent landmarks add a
diagonal block. The elimination-width cost follows from a bounded number of
neighbors per eliminated state block. Identical conditional Gaussian measures
have $\KL=0$, and exact posterior mass averages to prior-predictive coverage
by iterated expectation. Shape-dependent rejection, nonlinear conditioning,
or dense posterior fill need not preserve these conclusions.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Annular material implementation.}
The canonical neutral chart uses unconstrained offsets in
$\tilde{\xi}=(\theta,h,\eta_d)$, with
$d=d_{\min}+(d_{\max}-d_{\min})\sigma(\eta_d)$. Shared offsets cancel
at a common nominal station before the nonlinear world transport, using
the same reference radius to convert arc length to angle. Per-channel
scaling preserves the sparse CSC Kirchhoff/Mat\'ern-3/2 precision
construction and the raw latent generation--inference match. The annular
decoder reduces rejection against independent skin and bone queries;
empirical yield is not a containment guarantee, and acceptance
conditioning still changes the prior law.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional dynamic pullback evidence]\label{thm:pullback}
Condition on history, a fixed linearization and the association $m$. Let
$y=b_m+H_m x+J_q\Delta q+\epsilon$, where the centered Gaussian tracker and
echo innovations have covariances $\Sigma_q$ and $R_{\rm US}$ and are
conditionally independent of each other and of $x$. Then the linear-model
observation covariance is $R_{\rm eff}=R_{\rm US}+J_q\Sigma_qJ_q^\top$.
For a normalized effective likelihood $\bar R=R_{\rm eff}/\rho$,
$\rho\in(0,1]$, and information prior $(\Lambda,\eta)$,
\begin{align*}
 \Lambda'&=\Lambda+H_m^\top\bar R^{-1}H_m, &
 \eta'&=\eta+H_m^\top\bar R^{-1}(y-b_m),\\
 S_m&=\bar R+H_m\Lambda^{-1}H_m^\top, &
 e_m&=y-b_m-H_m\Lambda^{-1}\eta,\\
 \log p(y\mid m)&=-\tfrac12\big[e_m^\top S_m^{-1}e_m+
 \log\det S_m+k\log(2\pi)\big].
\end{align*}
Here $k$ counts geometric coordinates; appearance, clutter and association
factors must also have valid densities and priors on a common data measure.
To append such terms by simple multiplication, assume they do not depend
on $x$ conditional on the hypothesis and history. A latent-geometry merge
trigger can truncate the prior, and then exact evidence requires joint
integration; freezing a merge probability at a nominal geometry is an
approximation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the Jacobian of the full pullback residual, including any uncertain
frame and origin. Covariance addition gives $R_{\rm eff}$, including the
cross-echo blocks for a shared pose error. Completing the Gaussian square
proves the information equations; affine Gaussian propagation gives the
normalized marginal density. An OU transition with stationary covariance
$\Sigma_z$ uses multiplier $b=\exp(-\Delta t/1.5\,\mathrm{s})$ and noise
covariance $(1-b^2)\Sigma_z$, while the static shape block is unchanged.
Normalization for $\rho$ does not make the effective discount exact for a
correlated physical process. Nonlinear unknown landmarks, approximate
tracker models, and beam truncation are outside the exact claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Belief-IPE attenuation]\label{prop:ipe}
If $X$ is a Gaussian material contrast with mean $\mu$ and covariance
$\Sigma$, its integrated sine and cosine at frequency $p$ are the
corresponding functions at $p^\top\mu$ times $\exp(-p^\top\Sigma p/2)$.
A PSD increase of $\Sigma$ can only shrink this envelope, quadratically in
frequency in the exponent. With common-chart contrasts and intrinsic graph
features this representation is invariant to a common translation of
positions and anchor.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the real and imaginary parts of the Gaussian characteristic function.
Choose $p$ along scaled axes at frequencies $2^0,\ldots,2^5$; use integer
angular harmonics on circular coordinates. A common additive displacement
cancels in each contrast. For a random anchor the contrast covariance
includes the negative cross-covariance terms. Absolute IPE without
co-centering is not invariant, and attenuation by itself is not a bound on
prediction error.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint segment risk and embodied sufficiency]\label{thm:risk}
\textnormal{(i)} Let a frozen predictor and segment score be evaluated on
$n$ exchangeable calibration segments and a new segment. The closed joint
predictive set with cutoff at order statistic
$\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$ (or $+\infty$ if this exceeds $n$) has
miscoverage at most $\alpha$. If all realizations in that set pass the
geometric verifier, joint false certification has probability at most
$\alpha$; conditional risk among certified segments requires separate
selection-aware control.
An independent selected-segment audit of a frozen rule may use a one-sided
binomial upper bound on independent accepted audit units; clustered tapes
are not independent units.

\textnormal{(ii)} For balanced binary truth, a symmetric early experiment of
accuracy $p$, and a common remaining budget $T$, require continuation success
$s(T)$ for $m=\theta$ and $r(T)$ otherwise conditional on \emph{each}
evidence value. Assume established entries or impose this same symmetry on
complete per-cell success including establishment failure and cost. Then
the four-cell completion contrast is
$\Gamma_{\mathcal R}(T)=2(2p-1)(s(T)-r(T))$. The V8.3 matched physical defect
is $\Gamma_{\mathcal R}/2$ under the V8.3 reflection and no-reconnection
contract in the window $s=1,r=0$. If every successful legal policy has to
choose the correct branch at a precommitment stopping time, then
\[
 (1-\alpha)\frac{1+\mathcal I_{\rm in}(T)}2
 \le V^*_{\mathcal R}(T)\le\frac{1+\mathcal I_{\rm out}(T)}2
\]
for inner prefix experiments with both branches constructively feasible and
success at least $1-\alpha$ uniformly conditional on history and correct
branch, and outer prefix experiments covering all legal policies in scope.
Each $\mathcal I$ is the supremum of stopped-history TV in its family.
The history laws are defined strictly before branch execution and include
deadline-truncated no-commit outcomes with a marker. The inner guarantee
must hold on the full retained history law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), ranking the new score jointly with the calibration scores, with
random tie breaking for ranks, gives the miscoverage bound. False
certification requires miscoverage. For deterministic $W\succeq0$ the risk
Gram satisfies $\E[A^\top W A]=\bar A^\top W\bar A+
\E[(A-\bar A)^\top W(A-\bar A)]$, but it measures expected cost, not a
chance constraint. Pointwise calibration is insufficient: two independent
$.8$-coverage points have joint coverage $.64$.

For (ii), mode assignment uses only early evidence and independent noise.
The diagonal and off-diagonal cells therefore have means $ps+(1-p)r$ and
$(1-p)s+pr$. Interventions define each cell even if a deterministic policy
would not visit it. Matching and antimatching differ by $\Gamma/2$ in value;
matching and independent assignment differ by $\Gamma/4$. With no allowed
physical mode switch, projecting to mode gives a TV deficiency lower bound
of $2p-1$. Reflection of the allowed statistic reaches that lower bound for
the symmetric binary (or V8.3 Gaussian) experiment.
Here only the allowed control statistic, actual mode/configuration and
retained completion budget are matched; an immutable raw log is not among
the matched fields. A conversion can spend at most $T-t_0<d_0$ after
reserving completion time $t_0$, where $d_0$ lower-bounds reconnection time.
Classical statistical comparison alone can freely relabel modes; it does
not move the robot.
Finally, under the irreversible cut, success is contained in correct branch
selection. The binary Bayes rule on precommitment history has accuracy
$(1+\TV(Q_+,Q_-))/2$. Supremizing gives the outer bound. Bayes selection and
the certified continuation on each inner prefix give the lower bound by
conditioning on correct selection. A regime with wrong-branch recovery is
not by itself a witness for the cut.
\end{proof}
