# Anatomical GPS: theoretical foundations

Scope: these are model-level results for the proposed CT/atlas-to-ultrasound system. They do not establish transfer, clinical performance, or physical-robot safety. `verify_theory_math.py` supplies numerical witnesses and regression tests, not proofs. `theorems_tro_ijrr.tex` is the manuscript formulation.

## 1. Harmonic chart, shared drift, and metric-graph prior

Use a canonical harmonic chart `ξ=(θ,h,d)` with a local lift of the circular angle (or periodic features at the seam). A material-to-world map `Φ_{β,q}` transports a nominal point; `G_{β,q}` is its material displacement frame in millimeter units. The graph includes SFJ, CFA/CFV bifurcations, Hunter canal and popliteal landmarks; disconnected arterial and venous trees need not be artificially joined. Intrinsic edge progress is `τ∈[0,1]`. For edge `(u,v)`, `h_e(τ)=(1−τ)h_u+τh_v`, with independent initial `h_v~N(h̄_v,25² mm²)`. This is a longitudinal landmark model, not a replacement for intrinsic edge length. Enforcing longitudinal ordering truncates the joint Gaussian; configuration-specific lengths or a monotone reparameterization change the model and must be declared.

With vector-valued independent component priors in a common material trivialization, the first-order world model is

`c_e=Φ_{β,q}(ξ̄_e)+G_{β,q}(ξ̄_e)[u_s(h_e)+δ_e(τ)+g_e(τ,u_t;κ_e)+z_{e,t}]`.

At a physical junction, incident edges share the nominal endpoint and frame, the dynamic response value, and the OU state value. Equivalently, edge-specific dynamic terms can taper to zero at junctions. Otherwise the junction trace result applies to the relative prior, not the complete moving centerline.

Use Matérn-3/2 `k_s(r)=σ_s²(1+λr)exp(−λr)`, `λ=√3/180 mm⁻¹`, `σ_s=35 mm`. For the relative field use `L=κ²−Δ_G`, `κ=1/45 mm⁻¹`, with the nonnegative self-adjoint Kirchhoff Laplacian `−Δ_G`. The domain imposes a single trace at each vertex and zero sum of outward edge derivatives, with Neumann conditions at leaves. Covariance `a²L⁻²` corresponds to smoothness index 3/2 in intrinsic dimension 1. The spectral multiplier is `(κ²+λ_j)⁻²` on Kirchhoff modes of eigenvalue `λ_j`. Continuity has a pathwise interpretation; the flux condition can also be read in mean square via the edge derivative traces, not as a claim that paths belong to the operator domain. Fix `a` from the root of the reference knot-average marginal variance (target `σ_rel=6 mm`); marginal variance is not generally constant across a branching graph. See Bolin, Simas and Wallin, [Gaussian Whittle–Matérn fields on metric graphs](https://doi.org/10.3150/23-BEJ1647).

### Gauge cancellation and pose error

For companion vessels at the **same** `h`, form aligned residuals `r_e=G_e⁻¹(c_e−Φ_e)` using the true transport, or use the chosen common material chart to represent the centerline offsets directly. Then `r_a−r_b=(δ_a−δ_b)+(g_a−g_b)+(z_a−z_b)` exactly. A known nominal relative offset may be added to express a material constellation. Raw world subtraction contains `(G_a−G_b)u_s(h)` and cancels the drift exactly only when the compared transports agree (or after the indicated alignment). For mismatched stations there is also `u_s(h_a)−u_s(h_b)`. In a fitted chart the cancellation holds in the model; chart/tracking errors can leave residuals.

For a differentiable transport in a common output frame, the first-order pose perturbation of a difference is `[J_q(ξ_a)−J_q(ξ_b)]Δq`; if `J_q` is Lipschitz in `ξ` with constant `L_q`, its norm is at most `L_q‖ξ_a−ξ_b‖‖Δq‖` (plus second-order pose terms). This compares with `‖J_q(ξ_a)‖‖Δq‖` for one vessel. It is not an unconditional 10× improvement; the numerical gate demonstrates one nearby-point regime.

### Discrete precision and inference cost

Merge all endpoint knots incident to a vertex into a single DOF. Edge piecewise-linear basis functions give the stiffness matrix `K` (each length-`ℓ` element contributes `[[1,−1],[−1,1]]/ℓ`) and the lumped mass `D` (each endpoint receives `ℓ/2`). The discrete weak form uses natural zero *net* junction flux, with the balance equation also containing distributed load and mass. A generic piecewise-linear field sample is not asserted to have identically zero sum of the slopes of its first elements. The exact covariance of the discretized Gaussian with equation `(κ²D+K)δ=a D^(1/2)ε` (`ε~N(0,I)`) has precision

`Λ_G=a⁻²(κ²D+K)D⁻¹(κ²D+K)`.

The stored shared-chain knots are distinct and fixed independently of latent landmarks. Evaluating the stored field at moved landmarks belongs to the observation/geometry model, not the prior state definition. A Matérn chain defined at random landmark knots would have a Gaussian precision only conditional on their locations.

Using a consistent, non-diagonal mass matrix yields a different discretization and generally not this sparse precision. A continuous Matérn-3/2 chain can keep both value and derivative: with `A=[[0,1],[−λ²,−2λ]]` the exact transitions are `F_i=exp(A Δh_i)`, `Q_i=P∞−F_iP∞F_iᵀ`, `P∞=diag(σ_s²,λ²σ_s²)`. Multiplying consecutive transition densities gives a block tridiagonal precision; dropping the derivative generally makes the marginal precision dense. With the independent landmark block (and independent components), `Λ₀=blkdiag(Λ_s,Λ_G,Λ_h)` has linear nonzero count for bounded graph degree. Bounded elimination width gives linear factorization/solve cost in the number of state blocks. This holds for a suitably ordered fixed graph/tree and local factors preserving bounded width; arbitrary associations, fill from OU marginalization, or updates coupling many previously separate blocks do not inherit an unconditional linear per-frame bound. The cost must include the candidate hypothesis count and factor pattern.

### Generative–inference duality

Sampling from the same discrete normalized Gaussian and using its precision in inference gives zero prior KL on that latent block, conditional on the same profile, graph and source. For an exact, correctly specified joint model, any posterior set with mass `1−α` has that prior-predictive coverage by iterated expectation. This is not minimax coverage, per-anatomy coverage or finite-data generalization. Rejection for containment, projection, different profiles, or conditioning on latent topology changes the sampling law; zero KL does not extend to those accepted anatomies unless inference uses the same law. Matched raw draws and accepted/projected draws must be distinguished in experiments.

**Annular material implementation.** The canonical beta=0 annulus uses unconstrained offsets in `ξ̃=(θ,h,η_d)` and decodes depth by `d=d_min+(d_max−d_min)σ(η_d)`. Common nominal-station arc offsets, converted to angle by the same reference radius, and other common material offsets cancel exactly in aligned contrasts before nonlinear world transport (including the smooth finite-domain height map). Per-channel scales preserve the sparse CSC Kirchhoff/Matérn-3/2 precision construction; the same raw latent law is used by simulation and inference. The annular decoder reduces independently measured skin/bone rejection at large perturbations. Acceptance conditioning, if applied, still changes the law, and empirical yield is not a containment theorem.

## 2. Dynamic pullback and association evidence

External inputs are pose `q_t`, cardiac phase with `φ̇=2π f_HR`, and contact force `f_c`. Define the observed local coordinates by `G_probeᵀ(y_world−Φ_probe)` for an orthonormal probe frame. For uncertainty propagation use the derivative of the **complete** pullback/prediction residual with respect to pose (including moving origin and frame if these are uncertain). For independent zero-mean Gaussian tracker and US errors in the linearized model, marginalizing `q−q̂` gives exactly `R_eff=R_US+J_qΣ_qJ_qᵀ`. This is only first-order for nonlinear LBS and pose tracking. Stack all echoes with the shared tracker error to retain the resulting off-diagonal blocks. Lag bias or temporal pose correlation is not removed by a per-frame diagonal covariance inflation.

Exact covariance addition and the conditional information update require the two errors to be mutually independent, centered, and independent of the current state conditional on the history and hypothesis. When pose and anatomy uncertainties are cross-correlated, retain a joint conditional state and its cross-covariance instead.

The slow block stores shared/relative shape, landmarks and response parameters; the fast block has OU transitions `z′=b z+ε`, `b=exp(−Δt/1.5 s)`, `Cov(ε)=(1−b²)Σ_z` for stationary covariance `Σ_z`. Freeze interpolation and Jacobians at a stated linearization point, or condition on the nonlinear parameters. In particular `u_s(h_v)` for unknown `h_v` is not jointly linear-Gaussian without approximation. Given a discrete association hypothesis (including explicit null and merged branches), let the geometric measurement be `y=b_m+H_m x+v` with `v~N(0,R_m)`. If modeling a merged centroid this way, its weights are fixed conditional on the hypothesis. Null clutter and appearance provide additional likelihoods; they are not fictitious Gaussian vessel matches.

Set `ρ=clip(1−exp(−(Δs/8)²−(Δθ/.35)²−(Δf_c/2)²−Δt/2),.12,1)` with mm, rad, N and s units, and use the normalized observation model with `R̄_m=R_m/ρ`. This is an effective-noise heuristic, not exact marginalization of a correlated acquisition process. With prior `N(Λ⁻¹η,Λ⁻¹)`, completing the square gives

- `Λ′=Λ+H_mᵀR̄_m⁻¹H_m`, `η′=η+H_mᵀR̄_m⁻¹(y−b_m)`;
- `S_m=R̄_m+H_mΛ⁻¹H_mᵀ`, `e=y−b_m−H_mΛ⁻¹η`;
- `log p(y|m,H)=−0.5[eᵀS_m⁻¹e+log det S_m+k log(2π)]` for `k` geometric coordinates, with appropriate appearance, clutter and discrete transition terms.

All evidence branches need the same underlying observation measure. The Gaussian log normalizer must not be dropped when associations change dimensions/noise. These updates are exact only within the normalized, linear-Gaussian conditional model. Temperature weighting, pruning to a finite beam, any linearization, and independent treatment of temporally correlated evidence are separate approximations. An eliminated historical assignment cannot receive mass again without explicit replay or smoothing; priority to preserving alternatives rather than freezing old winners.

The additional discrete and appearance factors leave these closed forms intact only when they are state-independent given the hypothesis and history (or the conditional prior is otherwise known Gaussian). For example, the actual merge event depends on separation and can truncate the geometry prior; a scalar merge likelihood evaluated at the predicted geometry is an approximation. Exact inference would integrate such factors jointly with the geometric likelihood.

## 3. Belief-IPE and topological positional features

Use intrinsic graph spectral coordinates, signed distances to junctions, and a relative constellation in a common material frame (including `ttᵀ` to forget tangent sign). Fix signs/eigenspace conventions on the training reference graph; repeated eigenvalues do not individually define identifiable eigenvectors. Use no raw absolute position in the invariant reader. For IPE input, use posterior-decentered coordinates or coordinate contrasts whose reference transforms with the quantity measured. Co-center the covariance too: for `X−A` its covariance is `Σ_X+Σ_A−Σ_XA−Σ_AX`, unless the anchor is deterministic. This yields invariance to common translations of position and reference; raw IPE on an absolute `{θ,h,d}` with fixed origin is **not** gauge invariant. Scale each coordinate before forming phases with `p=2^j e_i`, `j=0,…,5` (respect angular periodicity on a circular coordinate).

For invariance to the full station-dependent shared drift, use `t=∂_h(r_a−r_anchor)` (or the derivative of an aligned same-station contrast) in `ttᵀ`, not the raw vessel tangent. `ttᵀ` by itself is invariant only to reversal of the tangent and constant spatial translations, not to derivatives of drift.

For `X~N(μ,Σ)`, `E exp(i pᵀX)=exp(i pᵀμ−pᵀΣp/2)`. Its real and imaginary parts give the cosine/sine features. Increasing `Σ` in PSD order attenuates every frequency's envelope; at fixed spatial direction the log-envelope scales quadratically with frequency. Decreasing posterior variance restores the corresponding frequencies. This does not prove that the learned scorer will avoid positional overfitting, or achieve millimeter accuracy. Those are evaluation hypotheses.

## 4. Whole-segment risk and embodied sufficiency

### T-RO: joint segment calibration

For random task matrix `A` and deterministic `W⪰0`, expanding around `Ā=E A` gives `G=E[AᵀWA]=ĀᵀWĀ+U`, `U=E[(A−Ā)ᵀW(A−Ā)]⪰0`. This is an expected quadratic cost, not a segment chance constraint. Marginal calibration/ECE cannot certify a segment or a selected subset: two independent points with exact .80 coverage each have joint coverage .64. Even a calibrated unconditional violation rate may concentrate in an accepted subgroup.

Instead, freeze the posterior and policy on training/dev. Use a score that covers the whole segment, e.g. the maximum across its verified locations and relevant geometric constraints of standardized residuals (computed with truth in calibration only). For `n` exchangeable calibration **segments** and one future segment, use the `k=ceil((n+1)(1−α))` order statistic, or `+∞` when `k>n`. The future score rank is uniform under random tie-breaking, so using a closed set gives marginal whole-segment miscoverage at most `α`. If the entire joint predictive set satisfies the geometric verifier, `P(violation ∧ certify)≤α`. This alone does **not** give `P(violation | certify)≤α`; the latter needs a selected-population guarantee. For example, fit the set and certification rule on independent data, freeze the rule, then use a fresh exchangeable audit of the selected segment population and report a one-sided upper confidence bound on its violation rate (or an independently calibrated selective-risk procedure). A finite grid needs a certified interpolation margin to imply a continuous-time guarantee. Correlated tapes from the same anatomy are not independent subject evidence. These conditions explain why marginal ECE and risk Gram inflation cannot fix the legacy `25/101` by themselves.

For example, apply a one-sided Clopper–Pearson bound only to independent accepted audit units sampled under the frozen deployment rule. If a participant supplies multiple tapes/segments, use independent participant-level units with a declared sampling rule or a dependence-aware bound at the relevant estimand, not a binomial bound on all frames.

### IJRR: V8.3 physical defect and V8.4 completion contrast

With balanced binary truth `θ` and a symmetric early observation `Y` of accuracy `p≥.5`, identifiability is `TV(P_{Y|+},P_{Y|−})=2p−1`. Graph inference supplies the early experiment but does not guarantee any particular `p`. Let the physical mode `M` be assigned using only `Y` and independent noise. For four legal interventional cells, take a common remaining budget `T` and continuation success `s(T)` if `M=θ`, `r(T)` otherwise, invariant to `Y` given `θ,M`. Then `c_diag=ps+(1−p)r`, `c_off=(1−p)s+pr`. The contrast is `Γ_R=c_{++}+c_{−−}−c_{+−}−c_{−+}=2(2p−1)(s−r)`. Matching and antimatched couplings have the same observation experiment and design-prior mode marginal, not the same conditional mode distribution. Value gaps are `Γ_R/2` (matched minus antimatched) and `Γ_R/4` (matched minus independent).

The cell continuation law assumes established entries at their common budget, or a symmetry assumption on *complete* cell success including establishment failures and costs. Conditioning only on successful establishment generally changes the experiment.

In the V8.3 witness, reserving completion budget makes physical mode switching impossible, and the allowed statistic can be reflected without moving the robot. The mode projection forces a worst-case total-variation residual of at least `2p−1`; for a symmetric binary channel the reflection attains it, because the leftover discrepancy is exactly the flipped observation distribution. The same holds for the symmetric Gaussian witness with `p=Φ(1/σ)`, since the two densities cross at zero. In the perfect-match/irrecoverable window `s=1,r=0`, the defect equals the matched-vs-antimatched value gap. It is not generally `Γ_R` or an equality for arbitrary experiments, transforms or logs.

The matched interface is the allowed control statistic, physical mode/configuration, and retained continuation budget. The immutable raw log is not among matched fields. In the source witness `t₀≤T<t₀+d₀`, so a conversion spending at most `T−t₀` cannot pay the reconnection time `d₀`.

For the cut, suppose **all** successful legal policies in scope must choose the correct branch at a stopping time before the deadline. Under equality of prior weights, branch choice from the legal precommitment history has accuracy at most the Bayes value `(1+TV(Q_+,Q_−))/2`. Supremizing over the outer family of legal prefixes yields `V_R*≤(1+I_out)/2`. For an inner family, if both branch programs are realizable and reliability is at least `1−α` conditional on every history and correct branch, Bayes selection followed by those programs succeeds with probability at least `(1−α)(1+I_in)/2`. The product contrast with `r>0` does not establish an irreversible cut; cut applicability needs a separate physical witness that wrong modes cannot finish in the available time. Native QP replay can test declared programs, not prove a bound for all physically admissible controllers without a separate reachability proof.

Define stopped histories strictly before branch execution and truncate at the deadline, retaining no-commit/failure markers. The inner-family guarantee must hold on the full retained law; filtering out timeouts would overstate the feasible lower bound.
