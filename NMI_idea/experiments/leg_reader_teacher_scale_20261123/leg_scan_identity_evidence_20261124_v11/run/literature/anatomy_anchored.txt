                                                             Yuan Bi∗ , Fangxu Han∗ , Ye Su, Yiping Zhou, Zhongliang Jiang and Nassir Navab



                                            Abstract—Ultrasound imaging is highly operator-dependent,
                                         motivating robotic systems that can reproduce expert scanning
                                         skills consistently. However, these skills involve coordinated
                                         interpretation of anatomical landmarks, ultrasound images, and
                                         probe-tissue interactions that are difficult to encode in manually
                                         designed control strategies. Imitation learning offers a promis-
arXiv:2610.32525v1 [cs.RO] 23 Oct 2026




                                         ing alternative, but its observation and action representations
                                         should reflect the anatomically structured nature of scanning.
                                         We propose an anatomy-anchored imitation-learning framework
                                         that integrates anatomy-guided probe placement with learned
                                         ultrasound navigation. The placement module identifies a task-
                                         specific anatomical starting point from external visual obser-
                                         vations and establishes an initial probe pose as the reference
                                         frame for subsequent scanning. A flow-based policy combines
                                         anchor-relative probe poses, ultrasound images, probe-mounted
                                         camera observations, and contact forces to generate scanning
                                         motions, while a termination head predicts task completion. This
                                         formulation avoids continuous reliance on third-person vision
                                         and reduces dependence on the robot-patient configuration. A
                                         handheld acquisition platform enables multimodal demonstra-
                                         tions to be collected during natural freehand scanning without
                                         a robot. On 16 previously unseen participants, task success
                                         rates reach 96%, 94%, 88%, and 94% for kidney, gallbladder,
                                         and liver standard-plane acquisition and radial artery sweeping,
                                         respectively. Compared with a fine-tuned π0 model, success on
                                         the abdominal tasks improves by 10-42 percentage points, with
