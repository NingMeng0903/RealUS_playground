## Implications for the new comparison

1. Execute the common MPC prescan through real simulated observation updates before cloning a public checkpoint. Earlier shared A/knee scans, anchor-only comparisons and two-capture checkpoints give different forms of initialization. Preserve the paid prefix in end-to-end cost and record its command and observation hashes.
2. Share the same public prior, sensor packets, legal physical moves, claim/stop interface, posterior support, progress and budgets with every arm. Test two-step live replanning and a genuinely longer belief search. A recurrent model using public posterior features should be identified as such and given equivalent prehistory warmup.
3. Keep randomized topology and evaluator identity out of public names, action IDs, action-order tie breaks, geometry feasibility, observation tuple order, and stopping. The shared motion bank may include prior hypotheses as long as it is independent of each case's actual anatomy. Use fresh post-checkpoint captures at physically downstream sites with two correctly identified samples on the same daughter and a proper no-target outcome. Require topology and fresh coverage in the combined endpoint, while reporting their component errors.
4. Check exact or joint witnesses for multidimensional caps. Rebuild all observed cost ledgers from the executed paths. Equal limits, equal frame counts, nominal windows and actual mean equivalence are distinct quantities. Distinguish shorter realized completion from strict two-sided cost equivalence. CPU can be reported as an additional ledger if it does not advance the synthetic clock.
5. A favorable result would remain evidence for one synthetic generative model and checked sampled motion. The original repository's `rm75_control/rm75_control/force/scan.py:62` is a low-level force-position streaming callback interface. It does not by itself validate a high-level MPC prescan, physical acquisition, continuous contact or image-derived topology in this experiment.
