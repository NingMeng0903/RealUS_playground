# Independent finite-area model v1

This is synthetic development geometry, not an estimate of a participant's tissue or probe calibration. It is frozen before held-out evaluation. The implementation and every run record the model/configuration hashes.

The face has 81 unilateral Kelvin–Voigt elements over x ∈ [−25,25] mm. The original TCP is the rotation pivot. Each element has its own positive stiffness; the total flat-contact stiffness is nominally 800 N/m, with seeded spatial variation. The hard-contact family multiplies stiffness by four. Element compression is the probe's rigid displacement minus surface height evaluated at the actual world contact point. Pressure is zero out of contact and otherwise the nonnegative spring-plus-damper force. Force and torque include the surface slopes in both translation directions and the rotated lever arms; no stiffness-center estimate is supplied to the controller. For a flat aligned surface only, the contact-side torque is −ΣxF. The physical environment-on-tool wrench has the opposite normal-force and torque signs. Its full power agrees with the stored spring-energy derivative, damping and friction; prescribed surface motion has a separately recorded external-power channel. Tool translation is rotated into world position consistently. This reduced plant supports three TCP translations and y rocking; unsupported angular commands are rejected. Tangential friction opposes measured scanning and rocking. Surface motion and command delay are explicit plant states.

The surface varies with actual path coordinate, not reference progress. Families include healthy, left/right edge recess, both edges, central recess, static acoustic shadow, intentionally wrong image direction, nonmonotonic acoustic coupling, curvature, moving surface, delayed execution and hard contact. All scenario streams use independent SeedSequence([scenario_index,seed]); the isolated mirror property test explicitly mirrors stiffness and geometry. Acoustic shadow and nonmonotonic response are independent of the mechanical pressure calculation. The both-edges family is a transient tracking test: an independent static equilibrium audit finds both required windows already feasible at zero rocking and 4 N throughout that family.

B-mode frames are rendered from the local physical coupling and seeded speckle. Disconnected rays have a shallow reflection and attenuated deep signal. The **same random-walk implementation** computes the controller's observation; direct plant contact truth never enters the controller. Test processing resolution is 32×48 at 25 Hz, with 152 ms image phase delay and a separate 6 ms processing delay. The frozen acceptance metric uses the fraction of mechanically coupled elements in each required window; both must reach 0.90. Independent acoustic availability additionally includes shadows and nonmonotonic dropout; acoustic gap/outcome labels and registered-image coverage use this additional truth, so a fully pressed shadow is never labeled a complete acoustic acquisition. Both metric families are reported separately. A central defect does not change the required-window metric and remains separately visible in diagnostic output.

Every comparator uses the shared production force/torque law with the effective ICRA force profile, 4 N target and 10 mm/s normal cap. A shared initial acquisition period precedes active scanning. All active scan transients count in force metrics. The nominal scan path is 60 mm at 20 mm/s; the supervisor expires at 4T+12 s. All algorithms use identical seeded initial conditions and exogenous time/space disturbances. Matched controls are generated from the paired full-controller run by the frozen rules in acceptance_v1.json.

Mechanical numerical admissibility includes bounded velocity, angular displacement and acceleration. These simulated limits do not certify the real robot's contact safety. Reports distinguish exact rigid geometry, contact-model assumptions, acoustic policies and experimental outcomes. A positive visual slack does not establish irreparability. Independent grid feasibility checks use plant coupling separately from the controller's QP and report mechanical limits and acoustic nonresponse separately.

Frozen held-out seeds and zero force margins are in acceptance_v1.json. Failed comparisons remain in the report; failure keeps the controller experimentally unaccepted and disabled by default. No physical robot is connected by any simulation or analysis command.
